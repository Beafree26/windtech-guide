GWO BST and BTT: the entry ticket

Most wind sites in Europe require GWO Basic Safety Training (BST): First Aid, Manual Handling, Fire Awareness, Working at Heights, plus Sea Survival for offshore work. Many employers also ask for Basic Technical Training (BTT), which covers mechanical, electrical and hydraulic basics.

  • You can do part of the theory online, but the practical part is always in person at a GWO-certified training centre (RelyOn).
  • Certificates are valid for 2 years, then you need a refresher (Vulcan Training Centre).
  • Prices vary a lot by country. Example: a 5-module offshore BST at RelyOn UK is listed at GBP 792 (source). Check current prices with each provider.

WINDA ID in 5 minutes

WINDA is the GWO training records database. Employers use it to check your certificates. Before your first course, create a free profile at winda.globalwindsafety.org and give the training centre your WINDA ID. Without it, your training records can't be uploaded.

CV tips that work in this industry

  • Put your certificates at the top: GWO modules with expiry dates, WINDA ID, driving licence, electrical qualifications.
  • Show hands-on mechanical and electrical work from any industry: maintenance, automotive, construction, ship work.
  • Say you're ready to travel and work rotations, and list your languages (English, German, Polish and Russian all help).
  • Write a short CV (1–2 pages) as a PDF named after yourself. No photos of turbines you didn't work on.
  • Apply to service companies and agencies as well as manufacturers. Many first jobs come through subcontractors.
